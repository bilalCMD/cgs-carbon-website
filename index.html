Background

Why particle size matters.

General background on the material we process. The notes below describe activated carbon as a material rather than any particular product or service.

  1. A porous carbon structure

    Activated carbon is a carbon material that has been treated to develop an extensive internal pore structure. That structure gives the material a very large internal surface area relative to its mass, and it is on that surface that adsorption happens.

  2. Contaminants adhere to the surface

    As a liquid or gas passes the carbon, dissolved and entrained substances accumulate on the internal surface. The more accessible that surface is, the faster the material can take up what it is meant to remove.

  3. Particle size governs access

    Fine material presents its surface quickly and is well suited to dosing and suspension duty. Coarser material is easier to retain in a bed and gives a lower pressure drop. Getting a carbon into the right size for its duty is a processing question — and it is the question this company works on.

Why does particle size matter for activated carbon?

Activated carbon works on its internal surface. Finer material makes that surface accessible more quickly, which suits duties where uptake needs to be fast. Coarser material is easier to retain in a bed and gives a lower pressure drop. The right size depends on the application the carbon is going into. This is general information about the material.

Glossary

Activated carbon terminology.

Short, general definitions of the terms used across this site and in the wider activated carbon industry.

Activated carbon
A carbon material treated to develop an extensive internal pore structure, giving it a very large internal surface area relative to its mass.
Activated lignite
Activated carbon produced from lignite, one of several raw materials used to make activated carbons.
Lignite
Sometimes called brown coal; a raw material from which activated carbons can be produced.
Activation
The thermal treatment that opens up the internal pore structure of a carbon material.
Adsorption
The accumulation of substances on the surface of a solid. Distinct from absorption, where a substance is taken up into the bulk of a material.
Adsorbate / adsorbent
The substance being captured, and the material doing the capturing.
Internal surface area
The total surface available inside the pore structure of a carbon, usually expressed per unit of mass.
PAC
Powdered activated carbon — fine material, generally dosed into a stream and then separated.
GAC
Granular activated carbon — coarser material, generally retained in a bed through which the stream passes.
Particle size distribution
The range of particle sizes present in a batch of material, rather than a single figure.
Grinding / milling
Size reduction of a material to a smaller, more uniform particle size.
Fixed bed
A vessel packed with granular material through which a stream is passed.
Dosing
Metered addition of powdered material directly into a stream being treated.
Pressure drop
The loss of pressure as a stream passes through a bed of material; finer material generally increases it.

Definitions above are general industry terminology, provided for reference. They are not specifications for any particular product.
